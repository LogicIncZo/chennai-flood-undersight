# MEDIA-CLAIMS.md — every public reference claiming success or impact of the RTFF & SDSS

**ChennaiFloodUndersight desk** · Compiled 2026-10-03 · Living register (append, never overwrite)
**Scope:** news reports, government statements, vendor/consultant posts, academic papers, and social discourse that *claim* the system works, helped, or will help. Every entry gets a claim type, the verifiable core, and a cross-check against our findings (F1–F10 in `REPORT.md`).

**Method:** web + X search sweep on 2026-10-03 (queries: RTFF/SDSS success-impact-accuracy; Ditwah December 2025 warnings; "Chennai Flood Monitor"; IFMC 2026; Tamil: வெள்ள முன்னறிவிப்பு சென்னை; vendor case studies). Not exhaustive — Google/X indexing is partial; this register grows with each activation.

**Claim types:** **T1** first-of-kind / launch framing · **T2** capability claim (street-level, 72-h, 5 models) · **T3** impact claim (save lives, reduce damage) · **T4** event-performance claim (a specific flood was forecast/handled) · **T5** expansion / re-announcement · **T6** vendor-consultant promotion · **T7** civic praise or criticism.

**Verification rule:** a claim is *verifiable* only if the outcome (forecast issued → warning reached residents → flood avoided/responded faster) is documented with dates against the public record. None is, today — which is F5 exactly.

## Pattern analysis (what "success" looks like in the media record)

**P1 — Every impact claim is prospective.** "Can save lives", "expected to significantly reduce damage", "will predict" — the entire corpus contains zero retrospective, dated, verified outcome (no "X streets were warned Y hours ahead; flooding was Z"). After ₹107.2 crore and one full NEM, the success genre has never left the future tense. *(ties to F5)*

**P2 — The first real test was met with silence.** During Ditwah (Nov 30–Dec 6, 2025) — the system's first NEM as a "fully operational" system — every warning credit in coverage went to IMD/RMC, CWC, collectors and GCC's ICCC. Not one article names RTFF & SDSS or Chennai Flood Monitor as the source of a warning that reached anyone. Absence is evidence when a system's entire public interface is press bulletins: the bulletins exist (we hold all 7), but no outlet treated them as news.

**P3 — One year later, the same promise is "news" again.** The Oct 2026 IFMC announcement re-offers street-level, three-day forecasts — the exact promise RTFF & SDSS was launched on in Oct 2025 — under a new centre and a new secretary's voice. Read with F4 (the public ward-depth layer is a static 2021 scenario), this reads as either an admission that the original promise wasn't delivered, or a rebrand. The register will track which.

**P4 — One handout, many outlets.** The launch wave (entries 1, 2, 4–13) repeats the same fact sheet — ₹107.2 cr, 4,974 km², five districts, seven vulnerable localities, TN-Alert — near-verbatim. This is syndication of a single press note, not 12 independent verifications. The only number any outlet could have independently checked (station freshness) went unchecked.

**P5 — The vendors write the victory lap.** Consultant (SECON-JBA), sensor (VEGA), pump (Grundfos) and geospatial (Naksha) accounts amplify "technological breakthrough" framing without performance data — the T6 genre exists because the T4 genre (verified event performance) doesn't.
